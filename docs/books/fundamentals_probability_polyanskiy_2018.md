## What this book trains

Yury Polyanskiy’s Fundamentals of Probability, with notes and problem sets that also credit John Tsitsiklis and other contributors on individual pieces, rebuilds probability on measure. A probability is a measure of total mass one. A random variable is a measurable function. An expectation is an integral. The bundle is twenty-six lectures, twelve recitations, problem sets, a midterm and a final.

The lectures run from models and conditioning through discrete random variables, abstract integration, product measure and Fubini, continuous distributions, moment generating functions, the multivariate normal and characteristic functions, modes of convergence, the laws of large numbers, uniform integrability, stochastic processes, Markov chains in discrete and continuous time, and martingales. The recitations practice sets, Borel–Cantelli, conditional expectation, Gaussian and gamma laws, the Jacobian formula, and coupling.

## Why it is worth knowing

Grinstead and Snell could say the law of large numbers with calculus. This course says it with a hypothesis you can check, and it distinguishes the modes of convergence: in probability, almost surely, in Lᵖ, and in distribution. Those are different theorems. Mixing them up is how a later paper exaggerates what a sample did. Uniform integrability is the extra ingredient that lets expectations converge when domination is awkward.

Characteristic functions turn a sum of independent random variables into a product, which is why the central limit theorem has a Fourier-analytic proof. Martingales are processes whose present value is the conditional expectation of the future. Optional stopping, prepared in the martingale lectures and in Gallager, is the reason a fair game does not become an unfair one just because you chose when to quit, under a hypothesis the theorem names. Markov chains here extend Grinstead’s finite chains to infinite state spaces and to continuous time.

## What it asks of the other books

Axler’s measure book (19), especially the probability-measures chapter, the integral limit theorems, and Fubini. The recitations include a background session on sets and real analysis, which is a repair, not a substitute for Lebl I (10). Grinstead and Snell (08) are the concrete course this one axiomatizes. Modern statistics (09) is the data-side neighbor.

García-Portugués and Molina (26) use the probability review and the limit theorems as the language of estimation. Gallager (27) continues Poisson processes, renewal and countable-state chains; it can be read beside the Markov and martingale lectures. The outline lists lectures in bundle order that is not numerical. Read them by lecture number.

## A walk through the chapters

### Lectures 1–6

Lectures 1 through 6 are the discrete theory on a measure foundation. Probabilistic models and probability measures, two fundamental models, conditioning and independence, random variables, and two lectures on discrete random variables and their expectations. Conditioning is a measure restricted and renormalized. Independence is a product of measures.

### Lectures 7–15

Lectures 7 through 12 build the integral and the continuous theory. Two lectures of abstract integration, product measure and Fubini’s theorem, two lectures of continuous random variables, and derived distributions. Derived distributions are the calculus of pushing a random variable through a map, the Jacobian formula the recitations practice. Lectures 13 through 15 are moment generating functions, the multivariate normal, and characteristic functions. A multivariate normal is determined by a mean and a covariance, and the covariance is a positive semidefinite matrix, Axler’s spectral theorem in probability’s clothes.

### Lectures 16–26

Lectures 16 through 19 are convergence: modes of convergence, two lectures on the laws of large numbers, and uniform integrability with convergence of series. Lecture 20 is the vocabulary of stochastic processes. Lectures 21 through 23 are Markov chains. Lecture 24 is infinite-state chains and continuous-time chains. Lectures 25 and 26 are martingales.

### 4. The recitations follow the same arc

The recitations follow the same arc: sets and analysis, Borel–Cantelli, conditional expectation, push-forward measures and a Fubini failure, sums of independent Gaussians and gammas, the Jacobian, characteristic functions, the strong law and empirical estimates, Markov chains, and coupling. Coupling is the technique of building two processes on one probability space so that a comparison becomes a pathwise fact. Twelve problem sets, with the twelfth optional, plus the midterm and the final, are in the bundle.
