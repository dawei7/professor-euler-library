## What this book trains

Casey Rodriguez’s Introduction to Functional Analysis is the Spring 2021 MIT 18.102 lecture notes, taken by Andrew Lin. The bundle is twenty-three lectures, ten homework sets, a midterm and a final. It is linear algebra with completeness required. A normed space in which every Cauchy sequence converges is a Banach space. The bounded linear maps from one normed space to another form a Banach space when the target is Banach. Subspaces and quotients, the uniform boundedness principle from the Baire category theorem, the open mapping theorem, and the Hahn–Banach theorem are the first named prizes.

The middle of the course builds Lebesgue measure and the integral on the line, explicitly so that the integrable functions become a Banach space containing the continuous functions. The end of the course is Hilbert space: inner products, orthonormal bases, Fourier series of square-integrable functions on an interval, the Riesz representation theorem, compact operators, the spectrum, and the spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators. The last lecture applies the machinery to a Dirichlet problem.

## Why it is worth knowing

Finite-dimensional linear algebra hides completeness because every finite-dimensional normed space is complete and every linear map on it is bounded. Infinite dimensions tell the truth. A subspace of a Banach space is itself Banach exactly when it is closed. A continuous linear functional on a Hilbert space is an inner product against one fixed vector. That is Riesz representation, and it is why a derivative of a norm and a gradient in a function space still have somewhere to live.

The joy is watching the finite-dimensional spectral theorem grow a hypothesis. A compact operator sends the unit ball to a set with compact closure. For a compact self-adjoint operator the notes recover an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Fourier series are the concrete case: the complex exponentials as an orthonormal set in L² of an interval, and the question of whether they are a basis. Axler’s measure book tells much of this story as a textbook. These lectures tell it in the order a course actually unfolded, including the homework.

## What it asks of the other books

Axler’s Linear Algebra Done Right (07) for inner products, adjoints and the finite-dimensional spectral theorem. Lebl I (10) for metric completeness, Cauchy sequences and the Riemann integral that these lectures declare not enough. Axler’s measure book (19) overlaps the outer-measure and Lebesgue-integral lectures and can be read before or beside them. The lectures construct the measure they need. They are not a gentler substitute for the textbook’s product measures and Lᵖ duality.

The pure and applied paths place this book after measure. The homework and the two examinations are part of the bundle, not a separate course.

## A walk through the chapters

### Lectures 1–3

Lectures 1 through 3 open the course and build Banach spaces, the space of bounded operators, and subspaces and quotients. A closed subspace of a Banach space is Banach. The quotient by a closed subspace is the infinite-dimensional form of a quotient you met in Axler and in Judson.

### Lectures 4–6

Lecture 4 proves the open mapping theorem after the uniform boundedness principle has been taken from Baire’s category theorem. A bounded linear surjection between Banach spaces sends open sets to open sets. Lectures 5 and 6 prove Hahn–Banach: a bounded linear functional on a subspace extends to the whole space with the same bound. One consequence in the notes is that for every nonzero vector there is a functional of norm one that attains the vector’s norm. The dual separates points.

### Lectures 7–13

Lectures 7 through 13 construct the integral the course wants. Outer measure, algebras and σ-algebras, Lebesgue measurable sets, measurable functions, the integral of a simple function, of a nonnegative measurable function, and of an integrable function, and then the completion that produces the Banach space of integrable functions. The motivation, stated in the notes, is a space of integrable functions that contains the continuous functions and is complete.

### Lectures 14–23

Lectures 14 through 16 pass to pre-Hilbert spaces, orthonormal bases, and Fourier coefficients. A separable Hilbert space has a countable maximal orthonormal set. Lectures 17 and 18 return from the exponentials on an interval to general Hilbert space and prove Riesz representation. Lectures 19 through 22 discuss compactness, compact operators, the resolvent and the spectrum, and the spectral theory of compact self-adjoint operators. Compactness in infinite dimensions is not the same as closed and bounded. Lecture 23 applies the theory to a boundary-value problem, the Dirichlet problem. The final examination, ten homework sets and the midterm follow the lectures in the bundle.
