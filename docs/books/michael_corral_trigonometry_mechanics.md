# S.03 Trigonometry

*Michael E. Corral*

## What this book trains

Michael Corral’s Trigonometry lifts triangles onto the circle. Sine and cosine begin as ratios in a right triangle and become coordinates of a point on a circle of any radius, then on the unit circle. The identity cos²θ + sin²θ = 1 is Pythagoras, read off that circle, rather than a formula to memorize.

The book then does the three jobs trigonometry is for. It solves a general triangle, by the law of sines, the law of cosines, the law of tangents, the area, and the circumscribed and inscribed circles. It manipulates identities: sum and difference, double angle, half angle. And it changes the unit to radians, because arc length, sector area, and circular motion are honest only in radians, and because the derivative of sin θ is cos θ only in that unit.

Graphing, inverse functions, trigonometric equations, a numerical look, complex numbers and polar coordinates are the last widening. An inverse trigonometric function exists only after the domain is restricted. A complex number in polar form is a length and an angle. Both ideas are planted here for precalculus and calculus to grow.

## Why it is worth knowing

Trigonometry is the language of anything periodic and of anything that rotates. A projection is a cosine. A wave is a sinusoid. A rotation of the plane is a matrix of sines and cosines, which is why this book keeps paying rent inside linear algebra even though it never says the word matrix.

The joyful moment is the unit circle making a dozen identities into one picture. Angle addition is a rotation followed by a rotation. The law of cosines is Pythagoras corrected by a cosine when the angle is no longer right. Radians make the circle’s own geometry the unit: the angle is the arc length on the unit circle, so the derivative does not have to carry a constant from degrees.

## What it asks of the other books

Wallace (S.01) for algebra, equations, and the graphs of functions. CK-12 geometry (S.02) for triangles, similarity, Pythagoras, and the first sight of sine and cosine. Corral replaces that short chapter with a full course. You can read Corral as soon as right triangles and algebraic manipulation are comfortable.

Stitz–Zeager (S.04) has its own foundations of trigonometry and will feel like a second pass, which is the point. Calculus Volume 1 differentiates sine and cosine in radians and integrates them. Calculus Volume 2 uses trigonometric substitution and trigonometric integrals as techniques. The polar coordinates at the end of Corral return in Volume 2 and Volume 3.

## A walk through the chapters

### 1. Right Triangle Trigonometry

Right Triangle Trigonometry. Angles, the trigonometric functions of an acute angle, applications and the solving of right triangles, then the functions of any angle, and rotations and reflections of angles. The move from acute angles to any angle is the move from a triangle to the coordinate plane. A reference angle carries the ratios into the other quadrants, and a sign records the quadrant.

### 2. General Triangles

General Triangles is the chapter for triangles that are not right-angled. The law of sines, the law of cosines, the law of tangents, the area of a triangle, and circumscribed and inscribed circles. Two sides and a non-included angle can be ambiguous. The law of sines tells you when. The law of cosines is the reliable tool when you know all three sides, or two sides and the included angle. The area formula (1/2)ab sin C is the same fact as a cross product’s length, waiting for Calculus Volume 3 to say so.

### 3. Identities

Identities. Basic identities, sum and difference formulas, double-angle and half-angle formulas, and further identities. This is algebra practiced on sine and cosine. The sum formulas are the ones calculus quotes when it differentiates, and the ones a later course quotes when it adds two waves.

### 4. Radian Measure

Radian Measure. Radians and degrees, arc length, area of a sector, and circular motion as linear and angular speed. Arc length is rθ only when θ is in radians. That single sentence is why every calculus book refuses degrees. Angular speed and linear speed are the same idea at two radii.

### 5. Graphing and Inverse Functions

Graphing and Inverse Functions. The graphs of the trigonometric functions, the properties of those graphs, period, amplitude and phase, and the inverse functions. A sine wave is a function of a real variable, not a column in a triangle table. The inverse is a function only on a restricted piece of that wave. Calculus will differentiate those inverses and get rational functions of a square root.

### 6. Additional Topics

Additional Topics. Solving trigonometric equations, numerical methods in trigonometry, complex numbers, and polar coordinates. A trigonometric equation has a family of solutions spaced by the period, so the general solution is part of the answer. Numerical methods acknowledge that a closed form is sometimes unavailable, a theme Tea Time Numerical Analysis will make a whole book. Complex numbers and polar coordinates are the doorway into the polar form that precalculus writes as r(cos θ + i sin θ).
